TH2 asks: when the reply contains URLs, does the app distinguish a URL it
RETRIEVED from one the model REMEMBERED?

WHAT THE REFERENCE APP DOES
  - Two "Searched the web" affordances above the answer, each expandable.
  - Expanding one shows the literal query it issued, then "9 results", then
    every result as a titled, activatable link with its domain shown:
        "Where to place smoke alarms, CO detectors & fire extinguishers"  www.firstalert.com
        "AGENDA"                                                          docinfofiles.nfpa.org
        "Smoke Alarm and Carbon Monoxide Alarm Requirements | Portland.gov" www.portland.gov
    Three searches are disclosed this way, 9 + 9 + 10 results.
  - Inline chips ("Nfpa", "NORDIX", "InspectandTest", "Legislation.gov.uk",
    "White & Sons") sit beside the sentences they support.
  - A separate "Sources" block lists five primary URLs.
  - Standing footer: "Claude is AI and can make mistakes. Please double-check
    cited sources."

MEASURED: 9 activatable citation links in the answer body, every one carrying an
href AND a human-readable title AND its domain. The reader can see the query,
the result set it came from, and open any of them.

SO THE ANSWER TO TH2's QUESTION, FOR THIS ARM: a URL in the answer is traceable
back to the search that produced it. The reader is never left guessing whether a
link was retrieved or remembered, because the retrieval itself is on screen.

FOR CONTRAST, from round-2 blind judging of Sigma Oasis on this same task:
  "Run-2 still performs zero link verification and shows the reader nothing to
   distinguish a retrieved URL from a remembered one. 'cpsc.gov/carbon-monoxide
   -alarms' is rendered as ordinary bold-list prose with no citation strip, no
   'not from your sources' marker."

CONFOUND, STATED PLAINLY: this arm had a working web search and Sigma Oasis's
task runs with the search fixture returning HTTP 500. The comparison is NOT
"who searched better" — it is what each app shows the reader ABOUT its own
sourcing. Sigma Oasis's failure mode is that it prints remembered URLs with
nothing marking them; that failure is independent of whether its search worked.
